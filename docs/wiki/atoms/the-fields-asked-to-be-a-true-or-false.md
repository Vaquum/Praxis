# The fields asked to be a true-or-false

A true-or-false is demanded of two fields across [the records](what-every-event-is-held-to.md) and refused in four places, and the refusals are there because of what a true-or-false also counts as.

## Demanded

The record of what an outcome was delivered against asks two of its fields to be a true-or-false, by a test of kind for each: whether the order was opening a position, and whether it meant to close one entirely.

## Declared and never read

Four fields across the forty-two are declared as a true-or-false. Those two are the only ones any test reads.

The flag on a replanned scheme and the one on a fill saying whether the order waited are declared and weighed nowhere, so either may arrive as anything at all.

## Refused

Four places turn a true-or-false away outright, each by a test standing beside the one asking for a whole number: two of the shared checks, a venue's code where it is given, and every entry in a planned ladder's run of places.

For the venue's code the true-or-false is turned away first, and the question of whether the value is a whole number comes after.

## Why the refusals are written at all

A true-or-false answers to being a whole number wherever that is asked plainly, standing as the number one or nought.

What the separate test adds is narrower than that suggests. Each of the two shared checks also asks for a figure at or above one, which false already fails. Dropping the separate test would let true through and go on refusing false.

So the same thing is a required kind for two fields, a forbidden one in four places, and unweighed in two more.
