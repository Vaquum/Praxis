# The two noughts a figure is compared with

[A test weighing a figure's sign alone](the-grounds-a-figure-is-weighed-on.md) compares it with one of two noughts, and which one it is decides what a figure of one kind does there.

## Which tests use which

Twelve of the eighteen compare against a nought the module declares once, as a figure.

The other six compare against a plain nought written at the point of the test. Those six are the counts — a scheme's slices and intervals, a bracket's timeout, and the place a scheme has reached.

## What it decides

A floating-point figure that is no number compares as neither above nor below a plain nought, so it passes.

Compared with the nought declared as a figure, the same value raises instead, and the building ends with a signal from the arithmetic.

## So

A count offered as a floating-point figure that is no number is accepted and written down. The same value offered as money is not — it stops the record being built, though by raising rather than by being refused.

The two readings sit in one module, and nothing marks which fields get which.
