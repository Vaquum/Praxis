# The grounds a figure is weighed on

[The records the trading side keeps](what-every-event-is-held-to.md) weigh a money figure or a count on up to three grounds, and which grounds a given field gets decides what gets through.

## All three

The fullest form asks the figure to be of the exact kind, to be finite, and to stand the right side of nought — three grounds in one test, the complaint naming all three.

## Kind dropped

Two records ask finiteness and sign and leave out the kind: the record of a sampled mark, and the record of money moving into or out of an account. A figure of another kind that answers both questions passes there.

## Sign alone

Eighteen fields across eight records keep the sign alone, among them a fill's amount, its price and its fee, a scheme's counts, and the figures an outcome reports.

An endless figure above nought passes, standing above it as any large figure would. One below nought is refused by the same test. Wherever finiteness is among the grounds, either is refused.

A figure that is no number cannot be compared: under the arithmetic settings a host runs with, the comparison raises, so the building ends with a signal from the arithmetic rather than with a refusal. A caller sorting failures by kind sees that apart from every refusal here.

[Which nought a test compares against](the-two-noughts-a-figure-is-compared-with.md) decides what a figure of one kind does there.
